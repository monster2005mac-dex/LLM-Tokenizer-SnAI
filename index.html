    Tokenization

    What's happening here?

    Computers can't read letters, only numbers. A tokenizer cuts text into pieces called tokens and gives each piece an ID. Byte-Pair Encoding starts from single characters and keeps merging the most common neighbouring pair, so frequent words become one token while rare words stay in smaller pieces.

    Vocabulary

    Every token gets an integer ID. This lookup table is what a model actually works with.

    What's happening here?

    The vocabulary is the model's dictionary: token → ID. More merges means a bigger vocabulary but a shorter token list for the same text. Common words get their own ID; rare words are built from smaller pieces.

    Embeddings in 3D

    Words used in similar contexts end up close together. Vectors are learned from your text (co-occurrence → PPMI → eigen-decomposition, the idea behind GloVe/word2vec), then flattened to 3D with PCA. Click any point.

    What's happening here?

    Each word becomes a list of numbers (a vector) learned from which words appear near each other, so words used in similar contexts get similar vectors. Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors: 1 means same direction, 0 unrelated. The 3D picture is a flattened view of those vectors.
